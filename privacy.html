Privacy Policy

What Fortune Apex collects, why it is used, and how to ask about your information.

Last updated: 10 October 2026

1. Information collected

Depending on how you use the website, it may store your username, display name, email address, password hash, account status, proposals, contact messages, and relevant account activity. Passwords are intended to be stored as password hashes rather than readable passwords.

IP addresses are logged. Security and administration audit records can include the connecting IP address, account identifier, event time, action, and related details. Examples include successful and failed sign-ins, installation events, and administrator or account-management actions.

The hosting provider may separately collect technical information in server logs under its own policies.

2. Why information is used

Account information is used to operate membership, sign-in, password recovery, proposals, moderation, contact messages, and community records. IP addresses and audit entries help investigate suspicious activity, reduce abuse, troubleshoot problems, and maintain accountability for administrative changes.

3. IP addresses and audit-log access

The application records the server-provided IP address for events written through its audit function. Authorised administrators who have access to the audit console can review recorded events. The interface displays the latest 100 events; this is a display limit and does not mean older entries are automatically deleted.

The application's rate-limiting table stores a SHA-256 hash of its rate-limit key, which may include an IP address, rather than the raw key. The audit log is separate and stores the IP address itself.

4. Passwords and recovery

Passwords are intended to be stored using password hashing. Password-recovery tokens are intended to be single-use and time-limited. Keep credentials and recovery links private.

5. Hosting and email providers

The site uses a third-party hosting service and may use an email provider for account notices, administrative notifications, contact workflows, and password-recovery messages. Those providers may process technical information needed to deliver their services under their own policies.

6. Retention

Audit records are stored in the website database. The current application does not automatically expire audit-log records on a fixed schedule; entries may remain until an administrator or database-maintenance process removes them. Records may be retained as reasonably needed for security investigations, dispute resolution, or accountability.

7. Requests and contact

Use the Contact page to ask about your information or request correction or removal. Some records may need to be retained for security, unresolved disputes, or accountability.

8. Security and community use

No internet service can guarantee perfect security. Use a unique password, report suspected account misuse, and avoid posting unnecessary sensitive information such as home addresses, school details, phone numbers, payment credentials, or personal secrets.

9. Policy changes

Material changes will be published on this page. This is a starter policy, not legal advice; review it against actual hosting practices and applicable requirements before launch.
